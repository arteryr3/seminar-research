01 • Introduction

Background of Study

SMEs play an important role in Malaysia's economy, contributing 38.4% of national GDP and 48.2% of total jobs according to the report's cited SME Corp. (2023) figures. As expectations around fairness and inclusion increase, gender equality has become an important workplace and governance issue.

Gender Diversity

Balanced representation of men and women across departments, roles and decision-making positions can support inclusive practices, different perspectives and better teamwork.

Governmental Ownership

Government involvement may influence SMEs through policy expectations, funding, governance requirements, training and institutional pressure.

ESG Performance

The Social component of ESG relates closely to fair treatment, diversity, inclusion and responsible workplace practices.

Sustainable Gender Equality

The dependent variable refers to long-term and consistent equality in opportunities, career advancement, participation in decisions and workplace culture.

02 • Research Problem

Problem Statement

Although gender equality receives increasing attention in Malaysia, the report identifies continuing concerns such as unequal pay, limited career opportunities and gender-based role patterns in SMEs. Some organisations may present equality initiatives without creating meaningful long-term change.

Research gap: The report highlights that there is insufficient evidence showing whether gender diversity, governmental ownership and ESG performance create genuine and sustainable gender equality in Malaysian SMEs.
03 • Objectives, Questions & Hypotheses

Research Objectives

  1. Examine the relationship between gender diversity and the sustainability of gender equality in Malaysian SMEs.
  2. Investigate whether governmental ownership supports gender equality sustainably in SMEs.
  3. Determine the effect of ESG performance on gender equality sustainability in SMEs.
  4. Identify the combined effect of gender diversity, governmental ownership and ESG performance on sustaining gender equality.
  5. Explore current practices and challenges SMEs face in maintaining gender-equal environments over time.

Research Questions

  1. How does gender diversity influence the sustainability of gender equality in Malaysian SMEs?
  2. How does governmental ownership affect efforts to maintain gender equality in SMEs?
  3. How does ESG performance contribute to sustaining gender equality in SMEs?
  4. How do gender diversity, governmental ownership and ESG performance work together to support sustainable gender equality?
  5. How do Malaysian SMEs face and manage challenges in sustaining gender equality over time?

04 • Literature Review

Theoretical Foundation

Stakeholder Theory

Businesses respond to expectations from employees, customers, regulators and communities. It supports the link between ESG performance and inclusive practices.

Institutional Theory

Government rules, industry standards and social expectations can shape organisational behaviour, supporting the governmental ownership perspective.

Resource-Based View

Gender-diverse leadership can be treated as a valuable organisational resource that brings different perspectives and supports governance quality.

Legitimacy Theory

Organisations seek acceptance by aligning actions with societal expectations; genuine gender equality can strengthen trust and legitimacy.

Social Role Theory

Cultural expectations about gender roles can influence behaviour and career progression, helping explain persistent barriers to equality.

06 • Research Methodology

Methodological Design

Approach

Quantitative
Structured questionnaire used to quantify perceptions and relationships among variables.

Design

Cross-sectional survey
Data are examined at a point in time.

Investigation

Causal / correlational
The report discusses relationships and proposed influence among variables.

Sampling

Convenience sampling
Target respondents are managers in Malaysian SMEs.

Data Collection

Online Google Form
Questionnaire divided into demographic and research-variable sections.

Analysis

SPSS + SmartPLS
Descriptive and inferential analysis with Structural Equation Modeling.

08 • Key Takeaways

What the Findings Suggest

ESG shows the strongest reported effect

ESG Performance has the largest reported coefficient among the three predictors (β = 0.447).

Gender diversity matters

Gender Diversity has a positive and statistically significant relationship with sustainable gender equality (β = 0.254; p = 0.005).

Governmental influence matters

Governmental Ownership also shows a positive and statistically significant relationship (β = 0.279; p = 0.023).

09 • Practical Implications

Implications for SMEs

  • Move beyond symbolic representation by embedding equality into hiring, promotion and leadership development.
  • Strengthen gender-inclusive workplace policies and monitor their implementation over time.
  • Integrate gender equality into ESG and social-responsibility practices rather than treating it as a separate initiative.
  • Use governmental support, training and policy guidance to strengthen governance and inclusive practices.
  • Encourage organisational cultures where equal participation and career development are sustained rather than temporary.
